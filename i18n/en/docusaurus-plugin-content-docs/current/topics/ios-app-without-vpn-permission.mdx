# iOS Networking App Without System VPN Permission

On iOS and iPadOS, private networking clients traditionally rely on Apple's `NEPacketTunnelProvider` (system VPN permission). While a system VPN routes device-wide traffic into a private network, it also introduces real friction: users must approve and manage a VPN profile, iOS only allows one active system VPN at a time, and enabling a personal mesh VPN immediately disconnects a corporate VPN, campus VPN, or network proxy.

The Larktun iOS and iPadOS app takes a different approach: it embeds Tailscale's user-space networking stack (`tsnet`) directly inside the application process. Your iPhone or iPad joins your Larktun, Tailscale, or self-hosted Headscale network **inside the app**, without requesting or occupying system VPN permission.

## System VPN vs. In-App `tsnet` Networking

Understanding the boundary difference helps you pick the right tool for the job:

| Dimension | System VPN Client | Larktun In-App (`tsnet`) |
|---|---|---|
| Network boundary | Device-wide (all apps matching route rules) | Strictly scoped to the Larktun app |
| System VPN permission required | Yes (`NEPacketTunnelProvider`) | No |
| Coexists with corporate / campus VPN | No (conflicts over the single VPN slot) | Yes (runs alongside existing system VPNs) |
| Encryption & path selection | WireGuard E2E + direct P2P / DERP relay | WireGuard E2E + direct P2P / DERP relay |
| Control-plane ACL enforcement | Per-device identity & ACL policy | Identical per-device identity & ACL policy |
| Best use case | Any third-party iOS app must reach LAN IPs | Focused remote admin: SSH, SFTP, Web UI, RDP, Ping, files |

## How In-App Networking Works Under the Hood

When you sign in to the Larktun iOS app, the embedded `tsnet` engine registers the app itself as an authenticated node on the control plane:

1. **Identity and policy sync**: The app authenticates using your Larktun account, an Auth Key, or a third-party Tailscale/Headscale auth key, and receives only the peer list and ACL rules authorized for that node.
2. **End-to-end encrypted transport**: All traffic uses WireGuard encryption between the app and target devices (home NAS, cloud servers, office workstations, or OpenWrt subnet routers).
3. **Direct P2P with automatic relay fallback**: The app attempts direct peer-to-peer NAT traversal first and falls back seamlessly to shared, dedicated, or user-managed DERP relays when strict mobile carrier NAT blocks direct UDP.
4. **Zero system network modification**: No OS routing table entries, DNS overrides, or global proxy settings are changed. Other apps on your phone keep using your normal Wi-Fi, 5G, or company VPN untouched.

## Built-In Capabilities Inside the App

Because the network stack lives inside the app, Larktun bundles the everyday tools engineers and NAS users actually reach for on mobile:

- **Device inventory**: View online status, private IPv4 addresses, and MagicDNS names across your servers, workstations, NAS boxes, and routers.
- **Ping & path diagnostics**: Measure round-trip latency and check whether the connection is direct or relayed before starting heavy sessions.
- **Built-in SSH terminal**: Connect directly to Linux servers, macOS hosts, or home mini PCs without exposing public port `22`.
- **SFTP file browser**: Inspect directories, pull log files, or upload configs on mobile without juggling a separate file-transfer app.
- **Private Web browser**: Open internal NAS dashboards (Synology DSM, ASUSTOR ADM), Home Assistant, Grafana, Swagger UIs, or AI Agent consoles directly over private IPs.
- **Remote desktop (RDP & WebRTC)**: Control Windows or Linux workstations from iPhone or iPad with H.264/AVC444 and RemoteFX support.
- **Third-party Tailscale & Headscale login**: Keep your existing Tailscale or self-hosted Headscale infrastructure and use Larktun strictly as your no-VPN mobile client.

## Why This Matters for Real Workflows

- **Emergency on-call ops**: Stay connected to your employer's VPN while opening Larktun to inspect a personal server, restart a container, or pull logs.
- **Clean security boundaries for AI Agents and internal tools**: Access stays explicitly scoped to authenticated sessions inside one app rather than routing background traffic from every app on your phone into a private network.
- **Zero profile friction**: Team members or family members can install the app and sign in without troubleshooting OS VPN profiles or split-DNS conflicts.

## When You Still Need a System VPN

In-app networking is intentionally scoped—it does not try to replace every system VPN scenario. If you need third-party iOS apps (such as Safari, an external media player, or a dedicated camera app) to reach private LAN addresses natively, use a system VPN client (for example, see [Log in to Larktun with the Tailscale iOS App](../getting-started/ios-tailscale-login-larktun)).

## FAQ

**Does skipping system VPN permission weaken encryption?**
No. `tsnet` runs the same WireGuard cryptography, key exchange, and control-plane ACL checks as a full client; only the traffic capture boundary moves from the OS kernel tunnel to the application process.

**Can I access LAN devices behind an OpenWrt or Linux subnet router?**
Yes. As long as the subnet route is approved in the console and permitted by your tenant ACLs, the Larktun iOS app can reach private LAN IPs behind the subnet router.
